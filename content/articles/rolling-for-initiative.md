The best nerdy ceremonies don't feel like a costume party. They feel like *the two of you*, with your shared language showing up at the moments that matter. Here are five ways couples can bring tabletop culture into a ceremony without losing the heart of it.

## 1. Open with a scene-setter

Instead of a formal welcome, begin with a short narration that sets the table: how you met, the campaign you've been playing together, the quest that led everyone into this room. Guests get a warm laugh in the first thirty seconds, and everyone relaxes.

## 2. Write your vows as bonds

Many tabletop games give characters a bond, ideal or flaw. Try writing a vow in that shape: *what I promise to protect, what I love about you, and the flaw I'll keep working on.* It gives sincere promises a structure that feels natural to you.

## 3. Make the unity ritual a shared roll

Candle lighting and sand pouring are lovely, but a unity ritual can just as easily be two dice in a single dish, or two players building a tower one block at a time. Pick something with a story behind it and I'll explain it to your guests as it happens.

## 4. Treat the rings as a party item

The ring exchange is already a moment where everyone goes quiet. A short line about where the rings came from, or what they're "enchanted" with (patience, mostly), keeps it personal instead of scripted.

## 5. Give your guests a role

A ceremony is more moving when the audience takes part. Ask your guests to answer a single line together, like "We will!", or to hold up a die when you exchange vows. A small job makes everyone feel like part of the party.

---

Start with what you actually love, then decide how loud to make it. The goal is a ceremony that sounds like you, and a nerdy detail or two is often all it takes.
